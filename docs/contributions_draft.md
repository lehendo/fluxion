## 2. Ordering: two options

**Option 1 (the current read): A, B, C, D, E, F, G.** Leads with attenuation.
**Option 2 (my suggestion; your call): B+G first, then C, D, A, E, F.** Rationale: order by robustness and by what survives scrutiny. B has a large, directly measured effect with an understood mechanism and a detection recipe; G is the spine and gives B and C their context; C and D are measured on 19 proteins and replicate across lags; A's lead status is exposed to three attacks listed in its weakest-point cell (prior art on the principle, the ratio being an identity, the original null not being explained by it); E may be falsified by 3c; F is hedged. TMLR does not require novelty, so putting the most defensible claims first costs nothing in acceptability, while a lead claim that a reviewer can dismantle in a paragraph (A as currently worded) costs credibility for the rest.
A can still be presented as the paper's theoretical contribution (its own section) without being the lead.
