# Fluxion — Novelty Assessment & Literature Review

**Date:** 2026-07-03
**Purpose:** Verify, before any paper planning proceeds, that Fluxion's core combination is not incremental and has not already been done. This supersedes the 2026-06-28 search recorded in project memory — the field has moved meaningfully in the five days between searches, which itself is informative (see "Strategic implications" below).

**The claim being tested:** a model combining (1) optimal transport conditional flow matching (OT-CFM) as the generative technique, (2) operating directly in the internal trunk latent representations of a *frozen*, pretrained structure-prediction model (Chai-1's single/pair reps) rather than 3D coordinates or a custom-trained autoencoder, (3) a DiT (Peebles–Xie diffusion transformer) architecture as the velocity network, (4) for generating *temporal* MD trajectories (a time series), not a static i.i.d. conformational ensemble.

## Verdict

**The four-way combination remains novel as of 2026-07-03.** No paper found — including a dedicated fetch of the April 2026 survey "Learning Structure, Energy, and Dynamics: A Survey of AI for Protein Dynamics" (arXiv:2604.25244), which explicitly concluded "DiT-style architectures appear absent from the protein dynamics literature surveyed" and found no method satisfying all four criteria — combines all four elements. Every close paper trades off on at least one axis: coordinate space instead of frozen latents, a custom-trained encoder instead of a frozen foundation model, diffusion/SDE instead of OT-CFM, or static ensembles instead of temporal trajectories.

**However, the surrounding space is far more crowded than the 2026-06-28 search found, and it is moving fast.** At least 8 new directly-relevant papers surfaced in this search that weren't in the prior list, several from the last few weeks. This raises the bar for what counts as a sufficient empirical contribution, even though the architectural novelty claim survives intact.

## Strategic implications for the project

1. **The architectural novelty claim survives**, but it is a narrower target than it looked five days ago. The distinguishing axes are now specifically: (a) Chai-1 (AF3-style, 256-dim pair rep) vs. everyone else's AF2/AF3-Evoformer or custom latents, (b) OT-CFM specifically vs. everyone else's plain diffusion, SDEs, or stochastic interpolants, (c) DiT specifically vs. everyone else's IPA/GRU/graph-network/Evoformer-derived architectures, (d) genuine temporal trajectory generation vs. the many methods that only produce static i.i.d. ensembles.

2. **BioKinema and TEMPO have already solved, in their own architectures, almost exactly the problem our pilot just discovered** (thermal noise dominates short MD lags). This is good news and bad news: good, because it independently confirms our diagnostic finding was correct and physically grounded, not a bug we're rationalizing; bad, because whatever fix we adopt (longer lag, explicit velocity conditioning, multi-scale) needs to be positioned as *our* application of a known idea within a novel architecture, not claimed as a new insight — reviewers who know this literature will have read both of these papers.

3. **The field's pace is itself evidence for the earlier risk assessment.** Five new closely-related papers in five days is consistent with what a Nature/Nature Methods reviewer would already know: this is a hot, fast-moving area with well-resourced competitors (Chai Discovery/OpenFold team on ConforNets, presumably-well-resourced teams behind BioKinema and TEMPO). Getting to a competitive empirical result before the field further consolidates around a small set of dominant approaches is a real, time-sensitive consideration — not a reason to abandon the venue target, but a reason not to delay reformulating and rerunning.

4. **BioEmu remains the benchmark to beat or match on standard metrics** (RMSF correlation, PCA/TICA projection overlap, free-energy landscape reproduction) whenever we get to evaluation — it's the field's current highest-profile result at this exact publication tier, and any Nature Methods submission in this space will be compared against it implicitly or explicitly.

## What this doesn't resolve

This review confirms the *idea* is not already done. It does not resolve whether Fluxion, once reformulated to use a longer time lag or explicit temporal conditioning, will actually produce a working, competitive model — that's still the open question from the pilot, unchanged by this literature review, and the reason the full paper design doc is being held until we have that result (per plan: option 2).
